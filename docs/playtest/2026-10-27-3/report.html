What works well

  • The report summary stays 44px tall at 1440, 768, 390 and 360px, with no horizontal overflow.
  • Phase accuracy precedes the error chart; full move grades are behind a disclosure.
  • White/Black lanes, hollow/solid points and grade colours identify the side and severity. Height uses a fixed 0–100 winning-chance-loss scale.
  • Charts have no permanent axis, lane or point text labels, per the owner's request. Hover, focus and tap reveal detail; click or keyboard activation selects the move.
  • Known linked players see + / − / = in Past analyses. Unidentified games retain the factual PGN result.

## Reproduced finding

### P2 — “Find great moves” gives no visible result after the reanalysis

**Steps:** Open Analyze → choose the saved game with result 0-1 → select **Find great moves** → wait for the engine to finish.
**Expected:** The action should show the great moves it found, or clearly say that none were found.
**Actual:** The app briefly shows “Reading the game…” and “Starting… / Queued”, then returns to the same report with the same visible move list and no result or completion status for the action. I could not tell whether it succeeded.

## Visual critiques

1. **Board dominates the report.** The board takes nearly two thirds of the desktop width, while the report itself is in a narrow right column. That makes the graph and move list feel secondary even though they are the report’s useful evidence. See [02-saved-analysis.png](02-saved-analysis.png).
2. **The report panel has substantial unused space.** The summary and report controls occupy only the upper part of a tall white column, leaving a large blank region below. A wider report layout could give the move list and evaluation more room without making the page feel empty.
3. **The evaluation graph is hard to read at a glance.** It is a short, low-height strip with small labels and a single dramatic change. The turning point is stated in text above, but the graph itself has little room to communicate move context. See [02-saved-analysis.png](02-saved-analysis.png).

## First-person journey

I opened `http://127.0.0.1:8765` at 1440×900 in an isolated Edge context. The signed-out Library immediately showed a clear Sign in action and prompted me to create or import a repertoire. ## Reproduced findings

### P2 — “New repertoire” starts with prefilled generic name

**Steps:** Sign in; open Library; select New repertoire.

**Expected:** A blank name field with an example/placeholder, or a clearly selected default that is easy to replace.

**Actual:** The name field contains `New repertoire`. The name is editable and creation works, but the generic value can be mistaken for an intentional name if a user submits without noticing it.

### P3 — First-time prompt conflicts with the account’s actual state

**Steps:** Sign in with the supplied reviewer account and read Library’s Next steps panel.

**Expected:** Guidance that reflects the state of the account, or no first-time checklist when the account already has repertoire and analysis data.

**Actual:** The dashboard reports `3 analyses · 1 repertoire`, while the Next steps panel says `Build your first repertoire` and marks it Done. This looks stale/inconsistent and lowers confidence in the checklist.

## Three visual critiques

1. The Library’s hierarchy is easy to scan: navigation, account summary, repertoire controls, then rows. Mastery and queue information are aligned as useful comparison columns.
2. The Create modal is compact and focused. The native White/Black selector looks like an ordinary form field, which is consistent but less visually prominent than the choice’s importance might warrant.
3. The empty repertoire editor has a large central chessboard and a small “Play a move to add it to this line” instruction. The board gives the screen a strong focal point; the instruction is understated and might be missed by someone unfamiliar with the workflow.

# Teams desktop review — 2026-10-27

## First-person journey

I opened Teams at 1440×900 in an isolated Edge context and signed into `reviewer-20261027@example.com`. The page began with **No teams yet**, a concise prompt, a **New team** button, and an Incoming shares empty state. I created `Review27 Team` by entering a name in a short dialog. The team opened immediately; I could see the owner role, one member (me), tabs for Members and Shared repertoires, and an Invite control. I did not use Invite or Add member.

I opened Shared repertoires and chose **Share a repertoire**. The dialog offered my own `Review27 Sicilian` and `Caro-Kann review`, then View only, Can copy, and Can edit. I selected only `Review27 Sicilian`, left View only selected, and shared it. The team panel then showed the repertoire, me as the sharing user, and the access choices.

To check team switching, I created `Review27 Study Group`, then selected each of the two team entries in the Directory. Each selection opened the matching team details; the first retained its shared repertoire, while the second showed no shared repertoires. I saved the isolated browser storage state for recovery.

## Functionality and ease

- Team creation succeeded with only a required name and opened the new team immediately.
- Selecting team entries in Directory switched the detail panel predictably.
- Sharing succeeded with View only, and the shared repertoire appeared in the selected team.
- The share dialog distinguished the repertoire and permission level. I could complete the share without inviting or contacting anyone.

I did not reproduce a functional defect in the exercised flows.

## Visual review

1. The two-column Directory and detail layout makes switching teams easy to understand. The selected team has a blue edge and pale blue fill, distinct from the white detail panel.
2. The team detail uses a compact header with owner badge and icon actions. The icons for rename and delete are visually quiet; they have accessible titles, but first-time users may need to hover to discover their purpose.
3. The Members and Shared repertoires tabs sit directly under the team header, and the active tab has a clear blue underline. The wide detail panel is spacious at 1440px, with some unused horizontal room in the one-member view.

## Not tested

- Invite link creation, link copying, and the recipient journey. I avoided any external invitation delivery.
- Adding/removing members or changing member roles.
- Can copy and Can edit sharing permissions, unsharing a repertoire, and permissions as seen by a different account.
- Rename/delete team actions, empty search results, incoming share acceptance, keyboard-only use, and responsive layouts.
- Persistence after signing out or using another account.

# Mobile Game Report retest

Date: 2026-10-27
Viewport: 390 × 844, touch enabled
Scope: Saved 0-1 game report

## First-person journey

I signed in with the disposable reviewer account and opened **Games** from the bottom navigation. That page showed game source controls and “No games checked yet”; it did not show Past analyses. I switched to **Analyze**, expanded **Past analyses**, and tapped the saved **0-1** game.

The report opened with the board, move strip, Coach / Engine / Report tabs, and a compact Coach summary. I selected **Report**. The stats appeared first in a vertically scrollable report pane: player accuracy and error counts, accuracy by phase, then the Errors chart and the collapsed Move grades disclosure. I tapped the White 2.g4 chart point. The board moved to 2.g4, marked the blunder on the board, and showed the point detail “White 2.g4 · Blunder · 41% winning chances lost.” I expanded **Move grades** and scrolled the report pane to view the grade key and counts.

## Reproduced findings

### Low — Past analyses is under Analyze, not Games

**Steps:** Open Games from the bottom navigation and inspect its visible sections; then open Analyze and expand Past analyses.
**Expected:** I expected saved analyses to be reachable from the visible Games/Past analyses area.
**Actual:** Games showed “Games from” and “Games to review” only. The saved analysis list was under Analyze → Past analyses. I recovered through that visible route and opened the 0-1 report.
**Evidence:** [02-games.png](02-games.png), [03-analyze.png](03-analyze.png), [04-past-analyses.png](04-past-analyses.png).

No failure reproduced in the report controls. Tapping the chart point selected the matching move and exposed its error detail. Move grades expanded and were readable after scrolling the report pane.

## Visual and density notes

- The initial Coach view is compact: summary, turning point, and next blunder sit below the board and move strip. The report tab presents statistics first, then the chart and detailed grades as the pane scrolls. See [05-report-summary.png](05-report-summary.png) and [06-report-tab.png](06-report-tab.png).
- On the Report tab, the board and navigation remain fixed while the report pane scrolls independently. This keeps move context visible, though the report gets only a short slice of the 844 px screen at a time.
- The Errors chart has no persistent lane or axis labels, as requested. Tapping a point reveals its move, side, grade, and winning-chance loss in a temporary detail label. See [08-chart-tap-blunder.png](08-chart-tap-blunder.png).
- Move grades are initially collapsed. Expanding them reveals the key and counts, but the newly opened content sits below the current scroll position, so I had to scroll further inside the pane. See [09-move-grades-open.png](09-move-grades-open.png) and [10-grades-visible.png](10-grades-visible.png).
